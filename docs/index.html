What the sweep shows

Below about half a metre of localisation error the two costs are interchangeable — IoU is marginally ahead, because requiring physical overlap rejects clutter that a 4 m distance gate lets through.

Past that, IoU falls away and cannot be tuned back. Its loosest possible gate is 1 − IoU ≤ 0.999, which means “admit any pair of boxes that touch at all”; there is nothing beyond it. For a 4×2 m car that ceiling is worth roughly a 3 m centre gate, and the extra metre a distance gate can reach — which no IoU threshold can — is worth +0.27 MOTA on the repository's sweep.

The usual explanation — that IoU stops working because true pairs stop overlapping — is not what is happening here. At the noise where IoU has already lost a third of its MOTA, only 3.7% of true (object, detection) pairs have zero IoU, and the cost still ranks the correct detection first over 99% of the time. The cost is not wrong; its support is bounded by the size of the objects.

Everything on this page is computed in your browser by docs/mot.js, a port of the Python package. CI checks the two against each other on every push: 33,851 comparisons, every assignment, track identity and ID-switch count identical, worst floating-point difference 3.6e−15.
